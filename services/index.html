What the work covers

UX content

The words and structure inside your product, from sign-up to settings, designed so people can get things done without asking for help.

Usually starts with

  • Onboarding that loses people before they start.
  • Error messages and empty states that haven't changed since launch.
  • A form or checkout people give up on halfway.

Includes

  • The words inside your product: buttons, labels, error messages
  • How everything's organised, so people can find what they need
  • Following someone's path through your product, step by step
  • Content everyone can read and use, including people on screen readers
  • The articles and answers people look for when they get stuck
  • The messages your product sends, and when it sends them

Content strategy

The plan behind your content: what needs to exist, who owns it, and how it stays consistent as the product and team grow.

Usually starts with

  • Different teams writing in different voices.
  • Content nobody owns, so nobody updates it.
  • A help centre or website that grew with no plan behind it.

Includes

  • The plan for what content needs to exist, how, and why
  • Taking stock of the content you have, and what state it's in
  • A written guide to how your business sounds, with examples
  • Checking what you say against how you actually sound
  • The shared reference that keeps everyone writing the same way
  • Who owns content, and how it stays up to date

AI experiences

Chatbots, assistants and AI features designed so people trust the answers, and know what to do when the AI can't help.

Usually starts with

  • A support bot customers try to get around.
  • AI-written content that sounds nothing like the rest of the product.
  • No plan for what happens when the AI gets it wrong.

Includes

  • The answers, prompts and fallback messages your assistant uses
  • Shaping how a back-and-forth with a chatbot or assistant flows
  • The rules and reference material an AI draws on to sound like you
  • Deciding where AI fits in how your team writes, and who checks what it produces

FAQs

How do I know if I need this?

If people get stuck, drop off or contact support about things your product should already explain, the words and structure are worth a look.

What is content design, and how's it different from copywriting?

The skills overlap. Copywriting says what your business wants to say, usually to persuade. Content design starts from what your audience needs to know or do, then shapes the words and structure so they can do it.

Who do you work with?

Any business whose product or service people need to understand before they can use it. That includes startups, scale-ups, charities and larger organisations.

We already have a designer or copywriter, why do we need this too?

Content design works alongside both. A designer decides how the product looks and works; content design decides what it says and how that's organised, so each role can focus on its own part. With copywriters, it keeps the product and the marketing sounding like the same business.

How much does it cost?

The Clarity Check is €300, and the full amount comes off any follow-on work booked within 30 days. Everything else is scoped and quoted after a first call, once it's clear what the work involves.

Do you offer one-off projects or ongoing support?

Both. We'll work out what's best for you on a discovery call, based on your goals and budget.

What do you need from me to get started?

Not much to begin with. Every project starts with a call to talk through your product, your goals and where things are getting stuck. After that, Content Content will need access to the product, any research or feedback you have, and someone on your team who can answer questions and make decisions. If some of that doesn't exist yet, that's fine. We'll work with what's there.
