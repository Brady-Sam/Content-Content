Why aren't your users coming back?

Your product has to make sense when you're not there to explain it.

Sam Brady, Founder of Content Content

You're well researched, well made, and ready to solve real problems for your customers, audience, users. You've got the GTM and you've spent the marketing money - here they come!

But wait, why aren't they sticking around? You did everything right. Right?

The people arrive, but maybe your product or service just doesn't quite make the sense it needs to, or at least not without you there to explain it. Would your product still work if you sent it like a deck, without presenting it?

You won't be in the room

While not exclusively a startup problem, it is where I tend to see this problem the most. Unfortunately it makes perfect sense as to why: the founder(s) has spent 100+ hour weeks building, perfecting and launching the business, so it becomes hard to see the experience with fresh eyes.

Customers don't have that context, the founder isn't there to guide them. They just want to get to that value and fast! In a 2026 Gartner survey, 67% of B2B buyers said they prefer to buy without talking to a sales rep, and 45% had used AI during a recent purchase.

Therein lies the fleshy side of this problem, that all businesses, no matter the size, have to deal with this - The User, a fallible human forever distracted, tired, and thinking about 101 different things at once. They need something easy to use or they're out.

Only as good as your word

Your product and service need to keep the promises all your marketing makes. Not just the big loud ones, but the small day-to-day ones like information that is accessible, intuitive and easily usable. This goes beyond a user interface to any digital content you've made to help your audience achieve their goals.

Check out the Shelter case study to see how marketing becomes overworked when you're not saying what your audience needs to hear, but only what you want to say. Campaign supporters wanted to follow the journey of the campaign, but the pages and information were not linked together and not telling the story the supporters needed to hear.

Centralising the content into one 'Hub' and rewriting it based on supporter feedback drove a 55% increase in traffic and a 40% increase in petition signatures.

Why you can't see it

This is a sneaky problem to solve, because it hides in plain sight. All the information you have isn't wrong, it's just maybe not right.

Psychologists call it the curse of knowledge. In a 1990 Stanford study, people tapped out well-known songs and guessed that listeners would name the tune half the time. Listeners got 3 out of 120.

So people leave because it seems like what you're offering isn't for them or they have to put too much effort in to make it worth their while. This happens in silence. Nothing is "broken" so you never think to fix it.

The signs to look for

Here are some potential tell-tale signs:

  • People don't come back after the first week. They couldn't find the value in the service because maybe they didn't know where to look.
  • Trials aren't converting. Parting with money is tricky. You have to really trust the brand and believe it will solve the problem you need solving. Inconsistent messaging and a steep price do not mix.
  • The same support questions keep coming up. This is a great one, because your audience is giving you free user feedback about exactly where the problems are.
  • Customers ask for things the product already does. Proves that you have the right solution, just that it is poorly explained.
  • Marketing has to keep pushing to get the same results. Time to look for those hidden and silent corners of the business. Poor UX copy is hiding in plain sight if you put yourself in your customers' shoes.

It's getting harder

The machines are feeling this problem too. Now more and more services are delivered via an MCP (a way for AI assistants like ChatGPT or Claude to use your product directly) directly in your AI of choice.

If the rules, tools, and descriptions aren't written with UX content best practices in mind, it's the humans that pay the price when the thing isn't working as expected and they're even further removed from the product.

How to check yours

The best way to check if something is up would be user testing - watching someone use your product unassisted for the first time. You don't need many people. Nielsen Norman Group found that testing with five users catches 85% of usability problems.

Another way is to create a copy doc or content-only wireframe of the page(s). Reading what they're saying without the help of design components, full layout, and branding will highlight how clear and useful your content is. Could you complete your goal with just the text alone? Because you should be able to.

Or do a side-by-side comparison of your marketing homepage and the first screen of your product. Are they using the same language? Is the product keeping the promises of the website?

"Submit your first invoice in 2 minutes." That might be what the person is trying to do, so don't bury this behind a long-ass workflow.

TL;DR

All the budget, time and energy spent on building your audience via marketing and outreach is wasted if your product is not intuitive, easy to use, consistent and designed with the users' goals in mind.

FAQ

How do I know if it's my product, not my marketing?

If the numbers are coming in but they're not staying and/or converting.

What's the difference between UX copy and marketing copy?

Marketing copy is selling and promoting your product, services, and solutions. UX copy is telling your audience how to use the product, services, and solutions. It is what they need to know, not what you want to say.

Do I need a content designer or a copywriter?

A content designer, UX writer or similar. They're experts at working out what information your audience needs and where they need it, then writing it so people can get things done. They'll also understand your product and the context of your business, so the words work for both sides.

It's a good idea to get a content designer on any AI interface you have too. It's words in and words out, so they'll be able to clean it up.
